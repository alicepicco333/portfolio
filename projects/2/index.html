Testing a model against its own requirements, and reporting what it cannot answer.

Brief
Cognitive biases are described in prose; there was no shared formal model to query them.
Role
Four of the sixteen ontologies (bias blind spot, confabulation, naive cynicism and naive realism), in a team of four; the 2026 explorer on my own.
Method
eXtreme Design: user stories, competency questions, ontology design patterns and alignment to Framester; in 2026, every competency question tested as SPARQL.
Outcome
16 OWL ontologies (119 classes, 52 object properties) and an explorer that walks through each story and tests all 47 competency questions: 37 are answered by the files.
Stack
OWL, RDF, SPARQL, Python (rdflib, owlready2), JavaScript, Oxigraph

Key decisions

  1. 01

    Run every competency question as SPARQL against the group’s OWL files, instead of trusting the documentation.

    Why
    Competency questions are the requirements an ontology has to answer.
    Result
    Only 12 of the 24 written queries answered their question as written; fixed and completed, 37 of 47 questions are answered by the files, 7 in part, and 3 ask about things the files do not record.
  2. 02

    Check language-model output against the domain before modelling from it.

    Why
    ChatGPT and Gemini were used early on for definitions, scenarios and user stories.
    Result
    It showed where their answers helped and where they had to be corrected.

The ontologies were built as a group project with Marco Lamorte, Corrado Consiglio and Salvatore Di Marzo for Knowledge Representation and Extraction (2022/23). In 2026 I independently built a visual explorer from the group's OWL files and documentation, and tested every competency question against them; the images here come from it.

Notes

Our group, Marco Lamorte, Corrado Consiglio, Salvatore Di Marzo and I, modeled sixteen biases, from the gambler's fallacy and the hot hand fallacy to pareidolia, naive realism and the blind spot bias. Each went through the same eXtreme Design workflow: research and documentation, ideation and design, modeling and implementation, then presentation and visualization. ChatGPT and Gemini were used early on for definitions, scenarios and user stories, and Framester among the resources for modeling.

The process is documented in a public GitBook. It showed how much understanding of a domain an ontology needs before any modeling starts, and where language models help and where their answers had to be corrected. In 2026 I built an explorer on the group's OWL files: it walks through each user story sentence by sentence as the graph fills in, compares any two ontologies, and runs every competency question as SPARQL in the browser. Testing showed what the documentation did not: only 12 of the group's 24 written queries answered their question as written, mostly because of reversed properties and typos. Fixed and completed, 37 of the 47 questions are answered by the files, 7 in part, and 3 ask about things the files do not record.
