Glove, Visor, Rover

A salvage worker in a sealed suit and thick gloves, his hands on a tool and his radio busy with the team, sends Junior, a probe rover, ahead with a squeeze, one hand and his voice, so he never has to walk up to a hazard himself. The setting is Proxima b as we imagined it: earlier inhabitants marked buried hazards in infrared that humans cannot see, and a fungus glows over them.

Method: listening, shapinglistening→ordering→counting→shaping→playing

Offline

Multimodal input designed as relations between channels, with fixed limits on what a language model may decide, prototyped on a physical car and in a 3D world.

Brief
Salvage workers on Proxima b dig in sealed suits and thick gloves among hazards marked in infrared they cannot see, and checking a site means walking up to it.
Role
A group project, in a team of four.
Method
A speculative world built on published research, a persona, weekly prototypes from a gesture car to the 3D Junior, and tests of the squeeze and gestures with three testers, plus logged response times.
Outcome
A prototype in two parts: the Arduino car, a two-hand gesture car that also takes voice commands, and the 3D Junior, which runs the whole system with one-hand gestures; a browser version is on this page.
Stack
Arduino, Python, MediaPipe, Whisper, Gemma 3 4B (Ollama), XTTS v2, force-sensitive resistor

Key decisions

  1. 01

    Narrow the whole suit to one rover and one glove.

    Why
    At the midterm the TAs said a whole suit “might be a lot”, and a rover keeps the worker away from the hazard: Junior goes up to it in his place.
    Result
    Hand, voice and squeeze each got a prototype of their own, week by week, before they were joined in the 3D Junior.
  2. 02

    Fixed rules act before the language model, and the model never moves Junior.

    Why
    A command that goes through Gemma, like “check that one”, takes a median of 2.3 seconds (62 logged commands): too slow for an emergency.
    Result
    “Stop” works by voice, fist and squeeze, a driving hand cancels the autopilot, and the model only proposes which glow to check.
  3. 03

    Let Junior speak in the cloned voices of the crew.

    Why
    After Sebeok's 1984 proposal to keep nuclear warnings alive by retelling them: Marvin could hear a warning in the voice of the teammate he lost.
    Result
    Built with XTTS v2: Junior answers three of us in our own voices, and anyone else in a blend of the three. A voice Marvin trusts also makes over-trust easier (Parasuraman & Riley, 1997).

The Arduino car

A voice test of the Arduino car on 8 October 2026, filmed from above on a floor at home: setting a base, then moving, stopping, turning and reversing the car by voice, and sending it back to base.

Spoken to the car

  1. Set base.
  2. Move forward.
  3. Stop.
  4. Turn right.
  5. Turn left.
  6. Go backwards.
  7. Move forward.
  8. Stop.
  9. Return to base.

Notes

What held up: every camera switch stops Junior first, and a work glove barely changed the gesture scores. Still open: a test with people who work in gloves rather than classmates, a wireless glove, a way home without drift, and consent for cloned voices.
