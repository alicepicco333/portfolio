Words of Gender↗

Words of Gender is the prototype of an archive of women authors. Taking its lead from Vera Gheno's Parole d'altro genere (Rizzoli, 2023), it asks how different authors, in different historical and cultural contexts, have written about the same concepts tied to gender: beauty, grace, strength, independence, marriage, maternity.

Brief
Women authors are under-represented in the literary canon and in course readings.
Role
In a team of four: the TEI encoding of Claude Cahun’s L’Androgyne and Simone de Beauvoir’s Mémoires d’une jeune fille rangée, and work on the website; the 2026 rework on my own.
Method
TEI XML excerpts linked to VIAF, Wikidata and WorldCat; 300 passages tagged with 27 concepts, 128 of them in a 2026 review of every occurrence.
Outcome
A reading edition of nine authors from 1558 to 1958 that follows each concept across texts.
Stack
TEI, Python, JavaScript, Wikidata

Key decisions

  1. 01

    Review every untagged occurrence of a concept word in context, and keep my tags apart from the original encoders’.

    Why
    The 2023 encoding left occurrences untagged, and the original team’s reading should stay visible.
    Result
    128 more passages tagged, with the reasons the other 59 were left out recorded.
  2. 02

    Highlight up to four concepts at once, never by colour alone.

    Why
    Colour alone fails readers who cannot tell the colours apart.
    Result
    Concepts can be compared within a passage without relying on colour vision.
  3. 03

    Translate all 127 French and Italian passages into English.

    Why
    So a concept can be followed across authors and languages in one reading.

Words of Gender began as a group project with Nicole Liggeri, Lucia Bertoldini and Chiara Parravicini for Digital Text in the Humanities. I reworked it independently in 2026; the version shown here is that rework.

Notes

Excerpts from nine women authors spanning five centuries (1558 to 1958), in Italian, French and English, are encoded in TEI XML and linked to VIAF, Wikidata and WorldCat. The 2023 encoding tagged 172 passages with 27 concepts; in 2026 I reviewed every untagged occurrence of a concept word in context, tagged the 128 that are instances of the concept, kept them apart from the encoders' own tags, and recorded why the other 59 are not.

The 2026 version turns the prototype into a reading edition: a reading view where up to four concepts are highlighted at once, never by color alone, with working English translations of all 127 French and Italian passages and the TEI of any passage on request; an author-by-concept matrix that opens every passage on a concept side by side; the concepts on a proportional time axis; a search across the texts and translations; and a timeline of first editions with each author's life and portrait from Wikidata.
