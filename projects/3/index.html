wipEU↗

wipEU is an open data project on how the representation of women in positions of power and decision-making correlates across the European Union, and how it has changed over time.

A research question answered with the data rather than illustrated, with a plain reading of how far to trust each result.

Brief
Data on women in EU decision-making is scattered across EIGE tables, and headcounts hide how far each body is from parity.
Role
Idea, data retrieval, visualization, quality and legal analysis, website; the 2026 rework on my own.
Method
EIGE datasets for six areas merged into mashups; shares computed from headcounts and checked against EIGE's own percentages; Spearman rank correlations across the EU-27 for every year.
Outcome
No area reaches parity; roles line up only loosely across countries, and in six of eight bodies the top level has fewer women than the level below.
Stack
D3.js, JavaScript, Python, EIGE open data

Key decisions

  1. 01

    Answer the research question with rank correlations for every year, rather than illustrate it.

    Why
    The project asked whether women’s representation lines up across roles and countries; headcounts alone cannot say.
    Result
    The answer is looser than the question hoped: in 2022 only two of twelve pairs of roles from different areas line up more than chance would give.
  2. 02

    Compare each body’s top level with the level just below it.

    Why
    A body can look close to parity overall while its top seats stay male.
    Result
    In six of eight bodies, women hold a smaller share of the top seats than of the level below.
  3. 03

    Recompute every share from the headcounts and check it against EIGE’s own percentages.

    Why
    Assessing the quality of the merged open data was part of the brief.
    Result
    The mashup datasets are released under CC BY 4.0.

wipEU began as a group project with Salvatore Di Marzo and Matteo Guenci for Open Access and Digital Ethics (2023/24). I reworked it independently in 2026; the version shown here is that rework.

Notes

We combined EIGE datasets across six areas (public administration, business and finance, politics, environment and climate change, education and research, law and institutions) into new mashup datasets, and assessed their quality, legal and ethical aspects as open data. In 2026 I redesigned the site around seats: a hemicycle for each body with one dot per real person, which can show a single country and the first year beside the latest. The site now answers its own question directly: rank correlations between six roles across the EU-27, for every year, with a scatter for each pair and a plain reading of how much to trust it. Alongside them sit a pace-to-parity projection, a country map and profiles.

The answer is looser than the question hoped: in 2022 only two of twelve pairs of roles from different areas line up more than chance would give, and one role, senior civil servants in environment ministries, runs the other way. What holds almost everywhere is a ceiling: in six of eight bodies women hold a smaller share of the top seats than of the level below. Final project for Open Access and Digital Ethics (2023/24, University of Bologna) with Salvatore Di Marzo and Matteo Guenci; I worked on the idea, data retrieval, the visualizations, the quality and legal analyses and the website. The mashup datasets are released under CC BY 4.0.
