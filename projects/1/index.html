Digitalian Folktales↗

Digitalian Folktales is a digital edition of tales from Italo Calvino’s Fiabe italiane, read alongside the dialect versions Calvino drew on: his Jesus-and-Peter cycles from Friuli and from Sicily.

Brief
Calvino retold Italy's folktales in standard Italian; the dialect versions he drew on are hard to read against his.
Role
Edition design and web development; the 2026 rework on my own.
Method
TEI XML with the dialect text, Italian and English aligned passage by passage; ATU types and a structural reading for each tale; Wikidata links.
Outcome
A searchable edition of 11 Jesus-and-Peter tales from Friuli and Sicily: 56 passages aligned in three languages and compared by tale type.
Stack
TEI, Python, JavaScript, D3.js, Wikidata

Research

Digitalian Folktales began as a group project with Nikolai Gorbachev. I reworked it independently in 2026; the version shown here is that rework.

Notes

Each tale is encoded in TEI XML: the dialect text, the Italian translation and an English working translation, aligned passage by passage, with a glossary of dialect words and narrators, collectors and places linked to Wikidata. For every tale the edition records its source, narrators, ATU tale type and a Propp-based reading of its places, people and outcome. In 2026 I added five Friulian legends from the magazine Pagine friulane (1890-94), including the origin of buckwheat, the subject of Calvino’s fourth Friulian tale, for which no source had been found, and three more Sicilian tales from Pitrè, all transcribed from the page images and linked to them.

The 2026 version makes the edition usable and comparable: a search across every passage in three languages, the TEI of any passage on request, maps of the places of collection, a table of the tale types told both in Friuli and in Sicily (the proud smith, St Peter’s mother, the jests of Christ and Peter), an Italian readability index, and a list of the dialect readings still to be checked by a speaker.
