A client commission taken from Figma to a working bilingual site, built around how visitors browse a label’s collection and its history.

Brief
A collection website for an Amsterdam label working across fashion, design and interior art.
Role
Design in Figma and front-end development.
Method
Collection and archive pages with filters, the label's history as a timeline, English and Dutch throughout.
Outcome
A bilingual site for the label's collection and archive.
Stack
Figma, React, TypeScript, Vite

Key decisions

  1. 01

    Keep the current collection and the archive of past pieces apart, each with its own filters.

    Why
    The label’s work runs from 1972 to today; current pieces should not be buried among fifty years of past ones.
    Result
    Visitors browse what is available now, or explore the label’s history, without one getting in the way of the other.
  2. 02

    One language switch for the whole site, rather than separate Dutch and English pages.

    Why
    Every page, filter and label exists in both languages, so switching should keep the visitor where they are.
  3. 03

    Compress every image to at most 1920 pixels wide, at near-lossless quality, before publishing.

    Why
    It is an image-led site served from a standard host.
    Result
    The photographs keep their quality at a fraction of the file size.

Notes

The site has a collection page of ready-to-wear pieces with filters, an archive of pieces from past collections, a history of the label told as a timeline from 1972 onwards, an about page and a contact page, all in English and Dutch.

Built with React, TypeScript and Vite from the Figma design.
