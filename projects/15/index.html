Culture4All↗

Culture4All maps access to culture across Italy: where libraries, archives and museums are, how far people live from a library, how libraries are used, and how reading habits differ and change from region to region.

Brief
Access to culture in Italy is unequal, but raw counts of libraries and museums mostly show where people live.
Role
Data visualization and web development; the 2026 rework on my own.
Method
ISTAT and Ministry of Culture open data, with rates per inhabitant, distances and 2023 population wherever the data allows.
Outcome
A data story: one municipality in four has no library, southern libraries lend a fraction of what northern ones do, and reading fell almost everywhere from 2011 to 2021.
Stack
D3.js, GeoJSON, Python

Key decisions

  1. 01

    Show libraries per inhabitant beside the raw counts.

    Why
    Raw counts of libraries and museums mostly show where people live, not how well they are served.
    Result
    The charts compare access rather than population size.
  2. 02

    Measure the straight-line distance from every municipality without a library to the nearest one.

    Why
    To test whether distance is what keeps people from libraries.
    Result
    It rarely is: the widest gap is in how libraries are used, with southern libraries lending a fraction of what northern ones do.
  3. 03

    Give every chart its finding in one sentence, a plain line on how to read it, and a data table.

    Why
    So the story can be followed without decoding the chart, and every number can be checked.
    Result
    A comprehension test plan for the charts is published with the code.

Culture4All began as a group project with Corrado Consiglio and Salvatore Di Marzo for Information Visualization (2023/24). I reworked it independently in 2026; the version shown here is that rework.

Notes

Built on open data from ISTAT and the Ministry of Culture, the 2026 version is a data story on unequal access to culture. It sets ISTAT's rate of libraries per 10,000 inhabitants beside the raw counts, measures the straight-line distance from every municipality without a library to the nearest one, and follows reading habits from 2011 to 2021. Readers can follow one region through every chart. Each chart states its finding, explains how to read it in plain language and has a data table behind it.

Distance is rarely the barrier: the widest gap is in how libraries are used, with southern libraries lending a fraction of what northern ones do, and reading is falling everywhere but Umbria. The data also has blind spots: regions that run their own heritage, like Sicily, almost vanish from the Ministry's events data. Made for Information Visualization (2023/24, University of Bologna) with Corrado Consiglio and Salvatore Di Marzo, who gathered and cleaned the data; I designed the visualizations and built the site, and reworked it on my own in 2026 with D3.js and Python. A comprehension test plan for the charts is published in the repository.
