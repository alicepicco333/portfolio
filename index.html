About

I design for information that’s complicated or easy to misread, so people can understand it, trust it and check it themselves.

That shapes how I work. Research comes first, from a background in cultural anthropology and digital humanities: interviews, usability tests, fieldwork and data analysis. I show the evidence behind what an interface claims: its sources, its data, how far to trust a result. And I test whether it worked, with people and with the data: in my Unobravo redesign, the usability score rose from 75 to 91.25 in a four-person summative test.

I've worked as a UX researcher and designer at BrainBite, an AI tutoring platform for children, on support for neurodiverse learners; done practice-based research as a visiting research fellow at the Institute of Network Cultures; and taken part in The Hmm's Voices of Makers program with DEN. My projects deal with museum collections, archives, public data and online therapy, and I care that the result is inclusive, well evidenced and easy to use.
